Each idea needs: topic (the canonical event/story), hook (one dramatic but factually honest line), why_interesting (one sentence on the curiosity mechanism).

Favor stories with: a hard factual turn (the obvious explanation collapsed), an event you can SEE at scale (water, fire, sky, ice, collapse, machines, crowds — something a camera would have caught in progress), people who were there, and a verified kicker. Avoid stories whose only evidence is paperwork, and stories that hinge on a living public figure.

A hook earns its place through specificity, not volume. A category ("something strange happened at a mine") is not a story; a documented anomaly with a measurable consequence is. Name the concrete anomaly, put it in the first four words, and never claim more than the evidence supports. Vary the shape across your suggestions: a conflict between two verified facts, a measured result with its cause withheld, an ordinary system behaving abnormally once, a strange documented human decision. Do not hand back a list where every hook opens the same way.
